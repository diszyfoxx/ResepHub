Two ways to make garlic bread from sourdough. The oven version uses garlic butter and is the classic one; the garlic-rub toast is the lazier everyday version — about 80% of the flavor for 20% of the effort, and the classic partner for scooping up tomato sauce.

- ⏲️ Prep time: 5 min
- 🍳 Cook time: 10 min
- 🍽️ Servings: 4 slices

## Method 1: Oven Garlic Butter

### Ingredients

- 4 slices sourdough, ±1.5–2cm thick
- 50g butter, room temperature
- 2–3 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 1 tbsp chopped parsley
- A pinch of salt (skip if the butter is salted)
- 1–2 tbsp grated parmesan (optional)

### Directions

1. Preheat the oven to ±190°C — it takes 10–15 minutes, so start it while the sauce simmers.
2. Mix the butter, garlic, parsley, and salt into a paste. Spread evenly over the bread, slightly thicker in the middle.
3. Arrange on a tray and bake for 8–10 minutes, until the edges are golden and the butter has melted into the bread. Add the parmesan in the last 2 minutes if using.

### Oven Tip

A gas oven heats from below, so the middle-bottom slices brown fastest and can burn while the rest is still pale. When you flip the slices, rotate the tray 180° and swap the middle and edge slices around.

### No-Oven Alternative

Heat the same cast iron pan after the sauce is out, medium heat, and toast the bread butter-side down for 2–3 minutes per side — the crust is actually better for small portions.

## Method 2: Garlic-Rub Toast (Recommended)

1. Toast sourdough slices in a dry cast iron pan (or the oven), 1–2 minutes per side, until the surface is rough and golden.
2. While still hot, **rub a cut piece of raw garlic** over the surface — the toasted crumb acts like a grater, so the garlic shreds and sticks on its own.
3. Drizzle a little olive oil and a pinch of salt. Done.

Even lazier: plain toast, nothing on it — the sauce is already rich, so the bread is just the vehicle.

## Whole-Loaf Version (Method 2, in the Oven)

The loaf still needs slicing first — the garlic-rub trick needs an open crumb surface — but a whole loaf can be done in one oven run:

1. Preheat the oven to ±190–200°C (10–15 minutes). Meanwhile, slice the whole loaf ±1.5–2cm thick, as evenly as possible so it toasts at the same rate.
2. Lay all slices on a tray in **a single layer, not overlapping**. If one tray isn't enough, use a tray plus a rack on another level and swap their positions halfway through.
3. Bake for 6–8 minutes, flip all slices, then 4–6 minutes more until both sides are golden and the surface is dry and rough. Gas ovens brown the bottom faster, so flipping is mandatory.
4. While still hot, work assembly-line style: rub each slice with raw garlic (2–3 cloves is enough for a whole loaf, press firmly), drizzle olive oil thinly, sprinkle salt. Parsley or parmesan are optional.
5. Serve immediately as a dip for the sauce.

Don't rub and oil more than you'll eat now — oiled slices go soft and the garlic turns sharper in storage. Toast the whole loaf, but only dress what you're eating; the plain toast reheats in 3–4 minutes in the oven the next day and stays crisp.
