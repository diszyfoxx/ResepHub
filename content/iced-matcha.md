Matcha served straight, with no milk or sugar to hide behind — so the one rule is to use **ceremonial grade matcha**. Without milk, culinary grade tastes harsh and astringent. Done right, the result is clean, naturally sweet (umami), and barely bitter — the tea version of an iced Japanese V60.

- ⏲️ Prep time: 5 min
- 🍽️ Servings: 1 glass (±300ml)

## Ingredients

- 3g matcha powder (±1½ tsp), ceremonial grade
- 60ml hot water, 75–80°C (never boiling — that is where bitterness comes from)
- 120g ice
- 120ml cold water

## Directions

1. **Sift the matcha** into a bowl through a small tea strainer so it doesn't clump.
2. Pour in the 75–80°C water and whisk fast in a zig-zag motion for about 20 seconds until a fine foam forms. Use a chasen if you have one; an electric frother or shaking in a small bottle also works.
3. Fill a glass with the ice and cold water.
4. Pour the matcha over it, stir once, and drink immediately.

## Adjustments

- Not intense enough → use 4g of matcha (a 1:75 ratio) or reduce the cold water
- Still bitter → drop the water temperature to 70–75°C; if it is still bitter, the grade of the matcha is the problem, not the technique
- Laziest method → **cold shake**: 3g matcha + 200ml cold water in a bottle, shake hard for 20 seconds. Cold extraction suppresses bitterness, so it tastes sweeter and cleaner — just less creamy than the hot-whisked version

## Variants

**Usucha (warm, classic)** — 2g matcha + 70ml water at 80°C. Whisk in a zig-zag until foamy and drink while the foam is still there.

## Notes

Don't let brewed matcha sit — it oxidizes and turns bitter within about 30 minutes.
