A simple tomato and minced beef sauce built for a small cast iron pan: the beef is seared first for browning, then everything simmers low and slow until thick. Serve over spaghetti with parmesan, and keep garlic bread on the side for wiping the plate — that's the best part.

- ⏲️ Prep time: 10 min
- 🍳 Cook time: 40 min
- 🍽️ Servings: ±2 pasta portions, or a dip for 3–4 people

## Ingredients

- 1 can Cirio tomatoes, 400g (peeled or chopped)
- 200g minced beef, around 15% fat for flavor
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ onion, finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 tbsp tomato paste (optional, deepens the color and flavor)
- ½ tsp sugar
- 1 tsp dried oregano or Italian seasoning
- Salt, black pepper, and a pinch of chili flakes (optional)
- 50–75ml water, to rinse the can
- Fresh basil and grated parmesan, to serve (optional)

## Directions

1. Heat the empty cast iron pan over medium-high heat until properly hot — a drop of water should sizzle and vanish instantly. Pat the beef dry with kitchen paper; this is what makes it brown instead of stew.
2. Add 1 tbsp of oil and the beef. **Don't stir right away** — leave it for 2–3 minutes until the bottom is browned, then break it up with a wooden spatula. Cook for 5–6 minutes total until no pink remains. Remove and set aside. Discard the excess fat, leaving about 1 tbsp in the pan.
3. Turn down to medium-low. Sauté the onion with a pinch of salt for 3–4 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and tomato paste, cook for 1 minute until the paste turns dark brick-red.
4. Return the beef, then add the Cirio tomatoes (crush peeled tomatoes roughly by hand or with a spatula first), the can-rinsing water, sugar, oregano, pepper, and chili flakes.
5. Simmer on the lowest heat for **30–40 minutes** with the lid ajar. Stir and scrape the bottom every 5–7 minutes — tomato sauce sticks easily to cast iron. If it gets too thick, add water a little at a time.
6. Season to taste: salt, and more sugar if it is still too acidic. Turn off the heat, add the basil, and rest for 5 minutes before serving.

## Serving

Toss with spaghetti or tagliatelle (160–180g dry for 2 people) and parmesan.

## Cast Iron Notes

A nitriding-seasoned pan handles acidity better than a regular seasoning, so a 30–40 minute simmer is fine. But don't cook tomato sauce in it for hours, and don't store leftover sauce in the pan overnight — transfer it to a container. After washing, dry the pan on the stove briefly and rub in a very thin layer of oil.

## Notes

- Refined coconut oil can replace the olive oil 1:1; virgin coconut oil works too, but its coconut aroma changes the character of an Italian-style sauce.
- Nutrition (estimated): the sauce alone is **±436 kcal per serving**.
