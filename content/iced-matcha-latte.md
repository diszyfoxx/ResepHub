An iced matcha latte with a clean two-layer look: cold milk and ice at the bottom, whisked matcha poured slowly on top. Stir just before drinking. The key is water temperature — matcha turns bitter and astringent above 85°C, so a kettle with a thermometer earns its keep here.

- ⏲️ Prep time: 5 min
- 🍽️ Servings: 1 glass

## Ingredients

- 2 heaped tsp matcha powder (±4g) — ceremonial grade if possible; it is bright green, while culinary grade is more bitter and dull
- 60ml hot water, 75–80°C
- 150–180ml milk (fresh milk or oat milk, both work)
- Ice cubes
- Sweetener (optional): 1–2 tsp sugar, honey, or simple syrup

## Directions

1. **Sift the matcha** into a bowl or glass through a small tea strainer. This is the step that matters most — matcha always clumps, and clumps will not dissolve by stirring alone.
2. Pour in the 75–80°C water and whisk fast in a zig-zag motion (not circles) until a fine foam forms on the surface, about 20–30 seconds. Use a chasen if you have one; a small whisk or fork works too, or shake it in a sealed bottle.
3. Fill another glass with ice to ¾, pour in the milk (dissolve the sweetener in the milk first if using).
4. Pour the matcha slowly over the milk and ice to get the two layers, then stir when ready to drink.

## Hot Version

Heat the milk to 60–65°C only (don't boil it), whisk the matcha the same way, and combine. Milk that is too hot kills matcha's natural sweetness.

## Nutrition (estimated)

Unsweetened with fresh milk: **±110 kcal** per glass. Two teaspoons of sweetener add about 40 kcal. Good-grade matcha already has a natural umami sweetness, so try it unsweetened first.
